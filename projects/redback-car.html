Redback Autonomous Vehicle

Overview

The challenge

The project objective was to design and manufacture a fully autonomous line-following vehicle capable of navigating a competition track while meeting strict constraints on size, weight, speed, cost, and reliability. The system needed to maintain accurate lane tracking at speed while remaining lightweight, durable, and manufacturable within a limited student budget.

Technical approach

The Redback vehicle used IR line sensors to detect the track boundary, feeding signals into a microcontroller that adjusted differential motor speeds in real time to maintain tracking. The chassis was designed around the sensor array geometry to minimise sensor lag during cornering.

  • IR reflectance sensors for line detection across track width
  • Proportional control loop for steering correction
  • Lightweight 3D-printed chassis to meet weight budget
  • Dual DC motors with independent speed control
  • Compact onboard battery pack sized for full-run endurance

Manufacturing

The chassis was manufactured using FDM 3D printing, chosen for rapid iteration speed and the ability to customise mounting geometry for sensors and electronics. Key dimensions were driven by sensor placement requirements and the motor mounting footprint.

Outcome

The Redback vehicle successfully completed the competition track autonomously, demonstrating reliable line tracking at competition speed. The project provided hands-on experience in embedded control, mechanical packaging under hard constraints, and manufacturing within a tight budget — all core engineering competencies.
