Overview

The problem

For AERO3110 (Aerospace Design 1), I was tasked with analysing the fuel systems of two reference transport aircraft — the McDonnell Douglas DC-9 and Boeing 737NG — to understand their design drivers, operational parameters, and system integration. This analysis would inform the development of an original conceptual fuel system meeting specific performance and redundancy requirements.

Reference aircraft analysis

The DC-9 and 737NG were selected as reference aircraft representing two generations of short-to-medium range transport design. Their fuel systems reflect different eras of engineering — from analogue pneumatic controls to modern digital fuel management systems.

McDonnell Douglas DC-9

  • Twin rear-mounted engines
  • Integral wing fuel tanks
  • Gravity and pressure refuelling
  • Manual fuel management

Boeing 737NG

  • Centre + wing tank configuration
  • Fuel feed redundancy via crossfeed
  • Automatic fuel balancing
  • Digital fuel quantity indication

Key design drivers identified

  • Fuel centre-of-gravity management during flight to maintain trim
  • Redundancy and failure isolation between engine feed lines
  • Vapour suppression and inerting requirements
  • Refuelling time and single-point pressure refuelling
  • Fuel quantity measurement accuracy and FQMS reliability

Original conceptual design

Drawing on the reference analysis, I developed an original fuel system concept addressing the specific performance requirements of the coursework brief. The design prioritised redundancy, CG control, and crew workload reduction through automated fuel management.

  • Symmetrical wing tank configuration with centre tank for extended range
  • Crossfeed capability for engine-out scenarios
  • Automatic fuel sequencing to manage CG envelope
  • Single-point underwing pressure refuelling with gravity backup

Outcome

The project demonstrated how reference aircraft analysis translates into informed conceptual design decisions. Understanding the rationale behind existing systems — their redundancy architectures, CG management strategies, and certification requirements — was critical to producing a credible original concept.
