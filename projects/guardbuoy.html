GuardBuoy

Materials & Competitor Analysis for an Autonomous Water-Rescue Device
DESN3000: Strategic Design Innovation

Project Snapshot

Year 2024
Duration 10 Weeks
Type Group Project (5 Members)
Role Materials Selection, Competitor Analysis
Tools Research, Benchmarking, Technical Communication
Outcome Pitch Result: 86%
Report Result: 88%
Project Overview

GuardBuoy is a conceptual autonomous water-rescue platform designed to improve swimmer safety through AI-assisted drowning detection and rapid emergency response. The system combines computer vision, autonomous navigation, solar power generation and marine engineering principles to provide lifesaving assistance in both patrolled and unpatrolled waterways.

My contribution focused on competitor analysis, materials selection and engineering justification to support the technical feasibility and commercial viability of the concept.

The Problem

Drowning remains a significant safety challenge across many coastal and inland waterways, particularly in environments where continuous human supervision is impractical or unavailable.

The project investigated whether an autonomous rescue platform could provide rapid emergency response while remaining technically feasible, economically viable and capable of operating within harsh marine environments over an extended service life.

My Role

  • Performed competitor analysis of existing rescue technologies.
  • Identified capability gaps and market differentiation opportunities.
  • Conducted materials selection for hull and enclosure structures.
  • Evaluated durability, corrosion resistance and manufacturability.
  • Developed engineering justifications supporting design decisions.
  • Contributed to the investor pitch and technical report.

Approach & Process

Concept Development

Initial concept sketches were used to define system architecture, rescue workflow and key subsystem integration.

Initial CAD

An initial CAD design was used to demonstrate the features and general layout of GuardBuoy.

System Visualisation

CAD renders were produced to communicate the design concept and support stakeholder presentations.

Testing Operational Visualisation

Initial Blender rendering used to evaluate the appearance and operational behaviour of the GuardBuoy concept.

Operational Visualisation

Final Blender animation demonstrating the completed GuardBuoy concept and intended rescue operation sequence.

Competitor Benchmarking

Existing rescue systems were analysed to identify capability gaps and opportunities for differentiation.

Materials Selection

Candidate materials were assessed against durability, corrosion resistance, manufacturability and cost.

Key Engineering Decisions & Trade-offs

Hull Material
Compared polymers, composites and metallic structures, balancing durability, manufacturability and cost.
Marine Durability
Assessed saltwater corrosion, UV degradation and long-term environmental exposure.
Impact Resistance
Evaluated structural robustness requirements for rescue operations and accidental collisions.
Manufacturing
Balanced ease of production against achievable structural performance.
Commercial Viability
Considered maintenance costs and lifecycle expenses alongside upfront manufacturing cost.

Results & Outcome

The project delivered a structured technical justification supporting the feasibility of the GuardBuoy concept while identifying suitable material solutions for long-term marine deployment.

The analysis informed both the engineering design direction and the project's business case, contributing to assessment outcomes of 86% for the investor pitch and 88% for the supporting technical report.

What I Learned

This project strengthened my ability to translate technical engineering research into clear and persuasive recommendations for non-technical stakeholders.

I gained practical experience evaluating engineering decisions through both technical and commercial lenses while improving my ability to communicate complex concepts in a concise and accessible manner.
